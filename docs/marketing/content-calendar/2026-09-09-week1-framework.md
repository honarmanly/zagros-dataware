## LinkedIn

```
Before spending more to acquire customers, run this five-point check.

Search — What does your ideal customer actually search for? Not your
company name or your internal terminology. The problem, need, or outcome
they're describing.

Presence — Where do they look? Google, maps, an industry directory, a
community, a referral. If customers look somewhere you don't exist, that's
a discovery gap, not a traffic problem.

Relevance — When they find you, can they tell in a few seconds who you
help, what problem you solve, and why it matters to them?

Trust — What evidence do they see? Examples, reviews, credentials, a clear
explanation of what you actually do. Don't assume trust — give people a
reason for it.

Action — Once someone's interested, what can they actually do? Call,
message, book, ask a question. If the next step isn't obvious, attention
doesn't turn into a conversation.

These five are connected. Strong visibility with weak relevance still
loses the customer. Strong trust with no clear next step still loses the
customer.

So before deciding you need more traffic, find where the journey actually
breaks. That's a better starting point for a growth decision.

Evidence over guesswork.
```

## Facebook

```
Is your business easy to discover? Run through this before spending more
on ads or a new website.

Search — would your ideal customer know what to search for?
Presence — are you visible where they actually look?
Relevance — when they find you, is it obvious what you do and who it's for?
Trust — is there enough evidence for them to believe you?
Action — is there an obvious next step?

Think of it as: find, understand, trust, act. If one of those breaks
down, more traffic won't fix it.

Which of the five would you rate lowest for your business?
```

## Telegram

```
A simple framework for diagnosing customer discoverability.

When a business says "we need more customers," the next question
shouldn't automatically be "how much more traffic do we need."

Start with five points: can the customer find you in the language they
actually use (search); are you present where that customer looks
(presence); do they understand the value the moment they land
(relevance); do they have enough evidence to consider you (trust); is
there a clear, low-friction next step (action).

The interesting part is that different failures need different fixes. A
search problem isn't necessarily a website problem. A trust problem isn't
necessarily an advertising problem. A conversion problem isn't
necessarily a traffic problem.

That's why diagnosis comes before the intervention, not after.

If you had to pick one of the five where your business is currently
losing the most potential customers, which would it be?
```
